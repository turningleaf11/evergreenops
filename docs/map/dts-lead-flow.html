Dashed nodes are real steps whose detail hasn't been captured — they are drawn so the hole is visible rather than invisible. Only one step in the entire flow is automated.

Stage register

Same pipeline, read as who-owns-what. The last line of each stage is what's missing there — captured beside the process rather than in a separate document nobody opens.

01

List build

Autumn DealMachine Manual

Lists built directly in DealMachine, filtered to SFR 1–4 unit. Records arrive already skip-traced, so there's no separate skip-trace spend or step.

Not capturedThe filter criteria used to build a list — which owner situations, equity positions, or distress signals define a good DTS list.
02

Dedupe and prep

Autumn LeadEngine Manual

The DealMachine list is downloaded and uploaded into LeadEngine, which dedupes it against what's already been worked and preps it for GHL.

FrictionDownload-then-upload is a hand-carried file between two systems. It's the kind of step that quietly doesn't happen during a busy week.
03

Push to GHL

Autumn LeadEngine → GHL Manual trigger

Send is pressed in LeadEngine and the prepped leads land in GHL, entering the outreach workflow on arrival.

04

SMS outreach

GHL workflow Automated

The GHL workflow sends SMS. A reply moves the opportunity's stage to Replied, which is the signal the Jr Acq team works from.

Not capturedWhat happens to a lead that never replies. No recycle, nurture, or long-term follow-up path is defined — so that volume currently has nowhere to go.
05

First contact and qualification

Jr Acq team Orbit interns Manual

The Jr Acq team owns the lead from Replied. They call, gather the 4 pillars, and set an appointment for the Closer.

Not capturedThe 4 pillars themselves, the appointment-setting workflow, and call dispositions — what each outcome is called and what it triggers next.
06

Closer appointment

Closer Manual

The Closer runs the appointment, digs deeper, and gathers the detail the Jr Acq call couldn't. Exits three ways: push to underwriting, push to follow-up, or disqualify.

Not capturedWhat distinguishes the three exits. This is the highest-leverage undocumented decision in the flow — it decides what underwriting ever sees.
07

Underwriting

Underwriting ARVA Cash — pending

The deal is underwritten and an offer is produced, then handed back to the Closer. This is the stage Cash is being built to screen in front of, so that obvious non-starters never consume an underwriter's time.

Not capturedThe underwriting process itself as a sequence — who does it, in what order, and what the offer hand-back actually contains.
08

Offer presented

Closer Manual

The Closer reaches back out to the seller with the offer.

Not capturedWhat happens on rejection — counter, follow-up sequence, or mark as loss. The accepted path is defined; the other one isn't.
09

Agreement

Closer Manual

On acceptance, the agreement is sent. This is where DTS hands off to the transaction side.

Sub-flows to map next

Named here so they exist as known holes rather than being rediscovered later. Each is its own page when we get to it.

  • Appointment setting How a Jr Acq call becomes a booked Closer appointment — scheduling, confirmation, no-show handling.
  • Call dispositions The outcome vocabulary for every call, and what each outcome triggers. This is what makes the whole pipeline measurable — without it there's no way to see where leads die.
  • Follow-up campaigns Both the "not ready" branch out of the Closer and the no-reply population from outreach. Currently the largest unowned volume in the flow.
  • The underwriting process Stage 07 as an actual sequence rather than a single box.
  • DTA and DTB Separate acquisition channels with their own flows, each getting its own page.
